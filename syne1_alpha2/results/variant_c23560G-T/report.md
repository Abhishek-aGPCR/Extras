## Bottom line

- **Position.** Human GRCh38 NC_000006.12:g.152176461C>A (chr6:152,176,461 C>A; GRCh37 chr6:152,497,596). In the giant transcript NM_182961.4 it is c.23560G>T, p.Glu7854Ter, exon 130/146 (base 100 of 167, 67 nt before the exon end, so not at a splice site), codon GAG>TAG (first codon position).
- **Giant isoform.** A premature stop at residue 7,854 of 8,797, in spectrin repeat 68. PTC 2,591 nt upstream of the last exon-exon junction (exon 130 of 146): nonsense-mediated decay is predicted. Any protein that escaped NMD would end at residue 7,853 and lack spectrin repeats 68–74, the LEWD kinesin-binding motif, the transmembrane segment and the KASH domain. It could not anchor at the nuclear envelope.
- **Nesprin-1α2 (muscle isoform).** Not in the α2 mRNA: the variant lies 8,149 bp upstream of the α2 transcription start (exon 1α2 in intron 130). The α2 mRNA and protein sequences are unchanged and NMD cannot act on α2 through this stop codon.
- **Other isoforms.** 55 of 81 annotated human transcripts contain the stop codon. All are predicted to undergo NMD, including RefSeq isoform 3 (NM_001347701.2, p.Glu56Ter) and variant 2 (NM_033071.5, p.Glu7783Ter). Isoforms that end before exon 130 or start after it, including all α2 transcripts, are not affected.
- **Measured data agree for the giant isoform but not for α2.** Holt et al. 2016 measured 78% less giant mRNA, as NMD predicts. They also measured 85% less α2 mRNA in patient myotubes, which this variant cannot explain through the α2 mRNA itself. Possible explanations are below.

## Why α2 mRNA could still fall (hypotheses, not tested here)

1. **Silencing of the whole locus by the premature stop (speculative).** PTCs can silence transcription of their own gene through chromatin changes (Bühler et al. 2005, Mol Cell, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molcel.2005.03.030). However, this was shown for immunoglobulin minigenes and not for several classic NMD reporters, and the authors suggest it may be Ig-specific. The α2 promoter lies in the same locus, 8 kb downstream of the stop codon. Nascent-RNA (e.g. PRO-seq) or ChIP across the locus would test this.
2. **Feedback from losing the exon-130 isoforms.** The variant removes the giant isoform and the other KASH isoforms that contain exon 130. α2 keeps its KASH sequence. Losing the giant isoform alters nuclear positioning and microtubule nucleation from the nucleus (Gimpel et al. 2017) and could feed back on genes switched on late in myotube differentiation, such as α2. The general differentiation markers (CKM, desmin) were normal, so any effect would have to be selective.
3. **Cell-line effect.** The data come from one immortalised clonal patient line compared with five control lines. A line-specific difference in late differentiation or α2 promoter use cannot be excluded without a second patient line or an isogenic correction.
4. **Not explained by NMD of α2.** The α2 mRNA has no PTC. Its sequence differs from the giant only in exon 1α2, and the variant is outside it.

### Zebrafish syne1a model of the patient allele (p.Glu7748*)

The residue aligned to human E7854 is syne1a Glu7748 (codon GAA), in exon 127 of syne1a-201. That exon sits directly upstream of the intron that holds the zebrafish exon 1α2, the same layout as human exon 130 and intron 130. syne1b carries Asp at this position and has no α2 exon.

**Design caveat for a zebrafish model.** An allele whose mRNA undergoes NMD can upregulate sequence-related genes in zebrafish ("transcriptional adaptation"; El-Brolosy et al. 2019, Nature, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1064-z). syne1b, or syne2a/syne2b, could therefore partly compensate in a syne1a p.Glu7748* line. Measure them, and compare with an α2-specific allele that deletes exon 1α2 (the strategy of Stroud et al. 2017 in mouse).

## Literature, checked

- **Holt et al. 2016, BMC Cell Biol 17:26** (https://doi.org/10.1186/s12860-016-0105-9). Reports: Immortalised myoblasts of a 16-year-old congenital muscular dystrophy patient, homozygous 'SYNE1 c.23560 G>T, p.Glu7854*' (paravertebral muscle), also written 'Nucleotide: 23560 gaa to taa; Protein: E7854X'. In patient cells nesprin-1-giant mRNA was 78% lower (myoblasts and myotubes) and nesprin-1-α2 mRNA 85% lower in myotubes than the mean of five controls; neither isoform was detected by immunofluorescence; myotube formation and CKM/desmin induction were normal. The authors note that the stop codon lies 5′ to the α2 first exon and found no simple explanation for the α2 decrease. Check: c.23560G>T and p.Glu7854* agree with NM_182961.3/.4. The codon is GAG, not 'gaa' as written in the methods: the change is GAG>TAG (amber stop). No known SNP makes the codon GAA, so this is most likely a typo; the protein consequence is the same. Data come from one patient line (n=1 donor) against five controls, so a line effect on α2 cannot be excluded.
- **Gimpel et al. 2017, Curr Biol 27:2999** (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2017.08.031). Reports: Same immortalised patient line ('homozygous nonsense mutation within the SYNE1 gene, nucleotide 23560 G>T'): nesprin-1 isoforms absent from the nuclear envelope, Pericentrin/AKAP450/PCM1 mislocalised, microtubule nucleation from the nucleus lost and nuclear spreading reduced. Check: Gives no transcript version; the number only matches NM_182961 coding (c.) numbering, consistent with the verification below. The original clinical report is cited as reference 16, which the PMC full text did not include and PubMed searches for E7854X / c.23560G>T did not find.
- **Attali et al. 2009, Hum Mol Genet 18:3462** (https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddp290). Reports: Autosomal recessive arthrogryposis caused by a SYNE1 intron 136 splice-acceptor mutation (A>G) with intron retention. Check: A different SYNE1 mutation; not c.23560G>T. Listed to avoid confusing the two.
- **Baumann et al. 2017, Eur J Hum Genet 25:262** (https://doi.org/10.1038/ejhg.2016.144). Reports: Homozygous nonsense variant in the last exon of full-length SYNE1 (distal arthrogryposis); the mutant mRNA was expressed at wild-type levels. Check: A different variant, but a useful contrast: a stop in the last exon escapes NMD exactly as the 50-55 nt rule predicts, whereas c.23560G>T lies 16 exons upstream of the last exon.

## Method and limits

- Positions come from RefSeq/Ensembl exon models on GRCh38 and the GRCh38 sequence. The engine checks the reference base before annotating.
- NMD is predicted with the exon-junction rule: a premature stop >50–55 nt upstream of the last exon–exon junction triggers decay. Exceptions are a stop in the last exon, a stop <150 nt after the start codon, and a stop in an exon >407 nt (Lindeboom, Supek & Lehner 2016, Nat Genet 48:1112). These are predictions; efficiency varies between cell types and transcripts.
- Domains come from UniProt Q8NF91, whose sequence is identical to NP_892006.3. They are carried onto other isoforms and species through shared codons and alignment.
